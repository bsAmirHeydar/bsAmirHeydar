## Selected Research Findings

These are working conclusions from my own research universe—not universal claims about markets.

1. **Trend-following has been more robust than many mean-reversion and counter-trend ideas I tested.**  
   The main difference was not headline backtest return, but stability when assumptions, regimes, markets, and timeframes changed.

2. **Prediction-heavy systems often create more fragility than edge.**  
   I moved increasingly toward participation/confirmation frameworks: define downside in advance, let the market confirm the thesis, and scale exposure only when behavior supports it.

3. **Failure outside the original sample is useful information.**  
   When a strategy breaks on another market, timeframe, or regime, I treat that as evidence of a hidden assumption to investigate—not as an invitation to add parameters until the backtest looks good again.

## Research Philosophy

A strong historical backtest is evidence, not proof. I care first about the mechanism behind an edge, hidden assumptions, regime dependence, execution costs, capacity, portfolio interaction, tail risk, and what would falsify the thesis.

Risk is part of the strategy itself—not a layer added afterward. My preferred architecture is hybrid: human judgment for framing, context, research direction, and assumption-challenging; systematic processes for sizing, execution, monitoring, and repeatable risk control.
